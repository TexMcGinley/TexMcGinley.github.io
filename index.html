01

How the week works

  1. 01

    A call.

    You describe the problem or the idea. I ask questions until I understand it well enough to scope it properly. If it is not a fit, I will tell you on the call rather than waste a week finding out.

  2. 02

    A scope, in writing.

    I send you one page: what the prototype will do, what it will not do, and what I need from you. Nothing starts until you agree with it. The week begins once we are aligned and I have everything I need to build.

  3. 03

    Seven days of building.

    I work remotely. You get one update partway through so nothing drifts off course quietly. Beyond what we agreed in the scope, I do not need anything from you during this phase.

  4. 04

    A review.

    I walk you through the working prototype, hand over the code, and we talk honestly about what it would take to turn this into something real.

02

Why I am not charging yet

Because I want the work more than the money right now. I finished six years of AI education and eight months building computer vision systems in industry, and what I am missing is a range of real problems, solved for real companies, under real constraints. That is worth more to me at this point than an invoice.

This is also how I find out what I want to build. I am not trying to be busy. I am trying to find the kind of problem I want to spend the next several years on, and the only way to find it is to get inside enough of them.

The free week is finite. I am doing it while I build a track record, and I am taking on a small number of projects, so I choose carefully.

04

Scope and fit

What I build

Web applications, internal tools, data pipelines, machine learning and computer vision systems, LLM-based tools, automations that replace something currently done by hand. If it is software and it solves a real problem, it is probably in scope. Ask.

If you are not sure what is possible, that is a fine reason to talk. I will look at how you work and tell you honestly where software would help and where it would not.

What I need from you

The person who owns the problem, on the first call and at the review. Not a middleman. I build toward one person's judgment of whether the problem is solved, and it has to be the judgment that counts.

Real data. Not a description, not synthetic samples. The whole point is that the prototype meets the actual problem.

A few hours, total. Questions answered during scoping, and attention at the review.

What this is not

It is a prototype, not a finished product. I will match your technology choices where it makes sense, so it reflects what a real version would look like, but it runs separately from your systems. It will not ship to your users or live inside your existing codebase. That is the next conversation.
